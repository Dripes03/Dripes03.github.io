Architectural Scene

Role Lighting Artist / Technical Artist
Tools Unreal Engine, Photoshop
Timeline 2 weeks
Category Lighting & VFX

Project Overview

An atmospheric lighting study featuring a classical European palace reflected in still water during golden hour. This project explores advanced lighting techniques, volumetric effects, and realistic water reflections to create a moody, cinematic scene.

Lighting Design

The lighting setup was designed to capture the magical quality of twilight:

  • HDRI skybox carefully chosen and color-graded for authentic sunset atmosphere
  • Volumetric fog with custom density and scattering parameters
  • Interior lights with realistic falloff and warm color temperature
  • Rim lighting from the sky to separate the building from the background
  • Reflection captures strategically placed for accurate water reflections

Water Simulation

The water surface required special attention to achieve realistic stillness while maintaining visual interest:

  • Custom water shader with planar reflections
  • Subtle normal map animation for micro surface detail
  • Color absorption based on depth for realistic water clarity
  • Screen-space reflections combined with planar reflections for optimal quality

Post-Processing & Color Grading

Extensive post-processing was applied to achieve the final cinematic look:

  • Custom LUT for color grading with emphasis on warm highlights and cool shadows
  • Bloom and lens flares for light sources
  • Vignette and film grain for atmospheric depth
  • Chromatic aberration subtly applied for lens realism

Challenges & Solutions

The main challenge was achieving perfect symmetry in the water reflections while maintaining performance. By using a combination of planar reflections for the main building and SSR for distant elements, I achieved the desired look without compromising frame rate.

Key Learnings

This project deepened my understanding of natural lighting phenomena and how to replicate them in real-time rendering. The importance of reference photography and understanding how light behaves in different atmospheric conditions proved invaluable.
